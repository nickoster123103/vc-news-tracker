Today's Climate & Trends

Today's flow of news captures a venture market running on two speeds simultaneously: private capital continuing to pour into AI and health-tech at a blistering pace, while the public and political mood toward AI curdles in real time. Three companies — Thatch, Profound, and Exein — either crossed or reaffirmed unicorn status today alone, underscoring how quickly capital is still being deployed even as public skepticism about the AI buildout hardens.

This continues a pattern visible across the trailing month. Crunchbase reported in the past week that 29 companies joined the unicorn board in August, led by AI software, robotics, and semiconductors, and that Y Combinator was the single busiest startup investor that month even as Nvidia ramped its own dealmaking pace. Today's headlines are a direct extension of that trend: Profound's jump to a $1.8 billion valuation just seven months after its Series C, and Exein's $270 million round at a $1.7 billion valuation, both fit the same script of AI-adjacent (or "physical AI") startups re-pricing at unicorn or multi-unicorn valuations within a matter of months. That velocity echoes recent nine-figure-plus raises flagged in Hacker News chatter over the past month — Temporal's $550 million round at a $12.55 billion valuation and Factory's $200 million raise at a $5 billion valuation — as well as TechCrunch's report last week that AI infrastructure challenger Cornelis raised $205 million explicitly to chip away at Nvidia's dominance. Capital is concentrating in fewer, larger, faster-repricing bets, particularly in infrastructure and applied-AI layers of the stack.

At the same time, the public-markets side of venture is telling a more cautious story. Crunchbase's "Hard Year for Software IPOs" piece notes that venture-backed tech companies have raised roughly $90 billion in domestic public offerings this year — the second-highest annual tally on record — yet the framing as a "hard year" suggests the exits that are happening skew toward energy, AI, and defense names rather than the broad software cohort that once anchored VC-backed IPO activity. That's consistent with the SEC's steady drumbeat of new S-1 filings over the past month, which has included crypto-adjacent listings (Hyperliquid Strategies) and a mix of SPACs, biotech, and financial names rather than a wave of marquee software debuts.

The more consequential thread today is political. Mark Zuckerberg's public jab at Anthropic over AI safety versus capability, alongside the Bannon-Sanders coalition condemning tech "oligarchs," and new NYT/Siena polling showing 61% of voters oppose data center construction, all point to a widening gap between the industry's internal pace-of-progress debate and a public/political backlash that increasingly transcends party lines. That tension was on full display when President Trump reportedly called into Jensen Huang's onstage appearance to dismiss AI safety concerns entirely — the same day Bill Gates's foundation pledged $1 billion specifically to combat AI-driven inequality. The result is an AI industry simultaneously accelerating its capital formation and product rollout (Meta's camera-free "Luna" smart glasses reveal being one such example) while facing a stiffening regulatory and reputational headwind, visible also in the Senate's blockage of the crypto-focused Clarity Act. For VC investors, the signal is that AI infrastructure and applied-AI bets remain well-funded and fast-moving, but political, safety, and public-opinion risk is compounding quickly enough that it may increasingly factor into diligence and exit timing.
